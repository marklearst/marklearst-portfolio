## Platform

## Users

The primary audience is peer design engineers and design systems engineers: the practitioners who read the code, adopt the open source, and vouch for people. The strategy is referral led, not funnel led. At the principal level, a peer's recommendation reaches a hiring manager more reliably than a recruiter screen does, so recruiter legibility and business outcome framing come second.

The consequence is specific. A peer has to be willing to forward the site to their own hiring manager, so it carries exactly one layer of legibility for a reader who isn't a peer, without turning into recruiter bait.

## Product Purpose

A portfolio for Mark Learst, design engineer and UI architect, working across design systems, design tokens, React, accessibility, and developer tooling. Open to principal design engineering roles. Success is an offer for a principal role, ideally in the design led product tooling cohort: Vercel, Linear, Notion, Apple.

## Positioning

Design engineer and UI architect who sets system direction in code.

The proof is the chain from idea to standard to tooling, each step dated and clickable: the Design Token Contract in W3C DTCG issue #220, the Variable Design Standard that formalizes it, and Primitree, which enforces it. a11y Companion, GlucoseIQ, and FigmaVars Hooks show the same habit in other domains.

Every claim has to survive a peer opening the GitHub sidebar. Nothing on the site claims adoption the numbers don't back.

## Conversion & proof

- **No single funnel.** Visitors arrive wanting different things: read a case study, install the open source, read the writing, make contact. All four paths stay open. The goal is a practitioner's respect, on the belief that the introduction follows.
- **The line a visitor remembers after ten seconds:** he sets system direction in code, and the site shows it working.
- **Belief ladder:** this person is real, not performing. The work goes deeper than the claim. I can operate the work, not just read about it. I want him on my team.
- **Proof on hand:** a11y Companion at 400+ users with production screenshots; Aurora at GM across four brands with 60% reuse, 30% velocity, and 50+ engineers trained; Primitree and GlucoseIQ published to npm, with GlucoseIQ running live in the browser; three named colleague testimonials; W3C Design Tokens Community Group membership and Arctic Code Vault contributions.

## Brand Personality

Precise, restrained, unshowy. Confidence shows through subtraction: the work carries the page, and nothing performs a trick for its own sake. The voice is a practitioner talking to practitioners: specific, unhedged, willing to name what was rejected and why.

## Anti-references

Four whole genres are out: the template developer portfolio (hero, skills grid, project cards, contact form), the generated SaaS landing page (gradient hero, feature triad, testimonial wall), the agency showreel (full bleed imagery, scroll theatre, vague deliverables), and the editorial treatment (display serif, italic headlines, drop caps, ruled columns).

Also banned by name:

- Tech bro register
- Uppercase tracked eyebrows above section headings
- Pulsing status dots and pill labels
- Monospace as decoration. Mono is for code.
- Terminal costume: fake prompts, fake paths like `../work`, and shell styled copy
- A named color scheme worn as identity. The site retired Monokai for that reason.
- Anything that reads as agent generated

The calibrated tell checklist lives in the design audit at <https://claude.ai/code/artifact/3b667ba6-4ad6-4e2b-bef1-bc76f69b9a9a>, with a local working copy at `docs/anti-slop-checklist.md` (untracked).

The neutral grotesque defaults (Inter, Geist) stay out for the same reason as decorative mono: both read as generated.

## Design Principles

**Earn the practitioner first.** The peer is the audience and the offer follows their referral, so depth beats legibility wherever the two conflict.

**Show, never assert.** Every claim on the site needs an artifact a reader can see or operate. A sentence describing a build failure is worth less than the failing build.

**The identity is what ships.** A dark canvas, Wotfard for everything people read, MonoLisa only where the text is code, sentence case headings, grayscale with one accent, and the braced wordmark. Only the decorative layer is negotiable.

**The site is the system.** The goal: every visible value comes from a governed token, and the site runs on the same open source it presents. GlucoseIQ already powers the glucose demo; Primitree takes over the site's tokens next.

**Practice what is preached.** An accessibility specialist's own site must pass the standard it sells. A contrast or focus failure here costs more credibility than it would anywhere else.

**Decoration earns its place or becomes exhibit.** Effects that demonstrate real craft get published as work with a name and a date, not run silently as chrome.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA, the site's own stated standard. Reduced motion is a hard requirement, not an enhancement, given how much of the interface moves. Color is never the only signal, and every interactive control has a visible focus indicator.
