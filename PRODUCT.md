## Users

Primary audience is peer design engineers and design-systems engineers — the practitioners who read the code, adopt the OSS, and vouch for people. The strategy is referral-led rather than funnel-led: at Principal IC level a peer's recommendation reaches a hiring manager more reliably than a recruiter screen does. Recruiter legibility and business-outcome framing are deliberately secondary.

The consequence is specific. A peer must be willing to forward the site to their own hiring manager, so it needs exactly one layer of legibility for a non-peer reader without becoming recruiter bait.

## Product Purpose

A portfolio for Mark Learst, Senior Design Engineer at ZoomInfo, working in design systems, design tokens, React, accessibility and developer tooling. Success in the next six months is an offer for a Principal Design Engineer role, ideally at a company in the design-led product tooling cohort — Vercel, Linear, Notion, Apple.

## Positioning

I build the tools design engineers use. Not a practitioner who consumes a design system, but the person whose specs, pipelines and widgets other design engineers depend on: the Variable Design Standard, Primitree, FigmaVars Hooks, a11y Companion, GlucoseIQ, and W3C Design Tokens Community Group contribution.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary and secondary CTA: deliberately not a single funnel. Different visitors arrive wanting different things — read a case study, install the OSS, read the writing, make contact — and all four paths stay open. The conversion goal is respect from a practitioner, on the belief that the introduction follows.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: he builds the tools design engineers use.
- Belief ladder: this person is real, not performing → the work is deeper than the claim → I can see the work, not just read about it → I would want him on my team.
- Proof on hand: a11y Companion at 400+ users with nine production screenshots already exported; Aurora at GM across four brands with 60% reuse, 30% velocity, 50+ engineers trained; Primitree and GlucoseIQ published to npm; three named colleague testimonials; W3C Design Tokens Community Group and Arctic Code Vault contributions.

## Brand Personality

Precise, restrained, unshowy. Confidence expressed through subtraction — the work carries the page and nothing performs a trick for its own sake. The voice is a practitioner talking to practitioners: specific, unhedged, willing to name what was rejected and why.

## Anti-references

Four whole genres are out: the template developer portfolio (hero, skills grid, project cards, contact form); the AI-generated SaaS landing page (gradient hero, feature triad, testimonial wall); the agency showreel (full-bleed imagery, scroll theatre, vague deliverables); and the editorial or magazine treatment (display serif, italic headlines, drop caps, ruled columns).

Also banned by name: tech-bro register, uppercase tracked eyebrows above section headings, pulsing status dots, and pill labels. Nothing that reads as agent-generated output. The calibrated tell checklist — twelve items, tuned for a dark monospace site rather than a light SaaS landing page — lives in the design audit at <https://claude.ai/code/artifact/3b667ba6-4ad6-4e2b-bef1-bc76f69b9a9a>, with a local working copy at `docs/anti-slop-checklist.md` (untracked).

The terminal and monospace identity is explicitly not an anti-reference. Research confirms it is the current house style of the target cohort, so it is the moat and is protected from any instinct to modernize toward Inter, Geist or a neutral grotesque.

## Design Principles

Earn the practitioner first. The peer is the audience and the offer is downstream of their referral, so depth beats legibility wherever the two conflict.

Show, never assert. Every claim on the site needs an artifact a reader can see or operate. A sentence describing a build failure is worth less than the failing build.

The identity is the moat. Monokai, MonoLisa, lowercase headings and the shell-prompt register are committed. Only the decorative layer is negotiable.

Practice what is preached. An accessibility specialist's own site must pass the standard it sells; a contrast or focus failure here costs more credibility than it would anywhere else.

Decoration earns its place or becomes exhibit. Effects that demonstrate real craft should be published as work with a name and a date, not run silently as chrome.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA, taken from the site's own stated standard and its listed values. Reduced motion is a hard requirement rather than an enhancement, given how much of the interface is animated. Colour must never be the only signal, and every interactive control needs a visible focus indicator.
