**♿️ a11y Companion v2.0.0 is live.**

If you want to track WCAG work inside Figma and FigJam without spreadsheet theater, this is for you.

Source checklist: [The A11Y Project](https://www.a11yproject.com/checklist/). Standards target: [W3C WCAG](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/).

![a11y Companion v2.0.0 release preview](/images/a11y_Compantion_v2.0.0.jpg)

## The part most tools skip

Most contrast tools lie by omission. They show a ratio and an AA or AAA grade, and they rarely tell you what they can't evaluate.

When I built the Contrast Inspector for v2, I decided it would only support what it can evaluate honestly.

- **Supported:** solid on solid, solid on a single gradient, and a single gradient on solid.
- **Explicitly unsupported:** dual gradients, image fills, and mixed or complex paint stacks.

If it can't evaluate a combination reliably, it says so instead of showing a ratio that looks compliant. Accessibility tooling is supposed to reduce false confidence, and a confident number on an image fill creates it.

## What's new in v2.0.0

- **Contrast Inspector** with on-canvas preview and gradient-aware checks
- **Light and dark modes** with six readable accent themes
- **Templates** for common checklist scopes
- **Markdown export** with WCAG level coverage summary
- **Avatar activity tracking** from real check and uncheck actions
- **Refreshed UI and faster performance**

Under the hood, v2 also includes:

- **Variable-first design-system guardrails** and baseline checks
- **WCAG contrast automation scripts** and nearest safe shade-step suggestions
- **Runtime contrast-safe accent resolution** for themed UI states
- **Canonical variable imports** and cleanup of legacy alias paths

## Guided checklist, not automated audit

The widget is a guided checklist for teams doing real reviews in the file, with shared context visible in Figma and FigJam. A ratio alone isn't a full accessibility decision. You still need people making the call, and clear limits on what the tool can and can't assert.

## Why I keep tightening this

Accessibility is personal for me. I've been the eyes and ears for my legally blind and deaf brother for decades, and I've watched him burn time on sites that don't care. That's why I built this, and why I keep tightening it.
